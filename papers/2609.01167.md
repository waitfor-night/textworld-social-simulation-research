## One-line takeaway

EpisodeSim uses persistent, classic-AI-style control state and a World Master to keep short social episodes coherent beyond locally plausible dialogue.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Bounded encounters such as restaurant lunches and hotel check-ins, with roles, scripts, material state, timing, obligations, commitments, and closure conditions.
- **Agents:** LLM-driven social roles coordinated by a World Master.
- **Observations and actions:** The World Master constructs scenes and exposes shared reality; agents propose role actions and receive adjudicated effects and updated obligations.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Complete an episode while preserving role constraints, commitments, delayed effects, and an explicit terminal condition.

## What the paper does

The architecture represents classic planning and state-tracking structures as natural-language control state interpreted by LLM calls. A World Master maintains authoritative reality, adjudicates proposed actions, tracks effects and obligations, and decides when the episode closes.

## Evidence and findings

In two held-out settings (three runs per condition), the authors report 0/6 major scene-level script, agenda, or closure failures with full EpisodeSim versus 6/6 with both minimal and grounded variants. The diagnostic is author-adjudicated and uses substantial per-scene API/token budgets; no independent raters or general benchmark are reported, so it is evidence for the design ablation rather than a measured general law.

## Reusable patterns

- Keep episode state, obligations, and closure outside the prose generator.
- Give one component authority to adjudicate proposed actions and delayed effects.
- Treat bounded episodes as replayable units with explicit start and terminal states.

## Limitations and open questions

The evidence is a small, author-adjudicated pilot limited to two settings, with no public implementation linked on the arXiv page. It remains unclear how the World Master scales to many agents, hidden beliefs, conflicting norms, or exogenous events, and whether natural-language control state drifts across long runs.
