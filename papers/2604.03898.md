## One-line takeaway

The work combines LLM-generated social posts and opinion interpretation with a small-world network and live event timelines to simulate attitude diffusion.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** A small-world social network exposed to real-world event timelines and retrieved news.
- **Agents:** 100 agents with multidimensional sociological beliefs.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents generate posts, interpret opinions, and react to social and external-event context.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Simulate the spread and evolution of attitudes; the abstract reports a 15-day Dublin anti-immigration-march case study.

## What the paper does

The simulator represents beliefs along multiple sociological dimensions, uses an interaction network, and incorporates event information retrieved from current news. It is accompanied by a Python package intended to make the simulator runnable.

## Evidence and findings

The reported case uses 100 agents over 15 days and focuses on an attitude-diffusion scenario. Because this is an early preprint and a narrow case study, it should be treated as an implementation lead rather than evidence of general predictive validity.

## Reusable patterns

- Separate durable beliefs from generated public messages.
- Make network topology and external shocks explicit inputs.
- Log both the message and the interpreted belief update.
- Keep live-news retrieval in the environment layer, with timestamps and source URLs.

## Limitations and open questions

The abstract does not establish human calibration, robustness across events, or sensitivity to retrieval errors. A live-news dependency also complicates reproducibility unless snapshots are stored.
