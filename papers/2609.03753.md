## One-line takeaway

SimSkill autonomously finds capability gaps in a traffic simulator, practices environment-grounded tasks, and stores reusable skills without changing the backbone model.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Simulation of Urban MObility (SUMO) with executable traffic workflows.
- **Agents:** A self-evolving language agent with an action-critic verifier and memory modules.
- **Observations and actions:** The agent generates tasks, runs simulator/code actions, checks artifacts, and consolidates episodic, procedural, and semantic memories.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Master a workflow over repeated exploration and transfer skills to held-out tasks.

## What the paper does

The system identifies missing capabilities, synthesizes and solves tasks grounded in SUMO, verifies results through an action–critic loop, and writes a reusable skill library. Experiments compare three backbone LLMs and two held-out benchmarks with independent artifact checks.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report up to a 25-point increase in verified completion and complementary contributions from procedural and semantic memory. They also report that gains depend on the backbone and inference budget; memory does not uniformly reduce cost or improve every model.

## Reusable patterns

- Couple natural-language planning with executable tools and artifact verification.
- Separate episodic traces from procedural and semantic abstractions.
- Let an agent generate its own curriculum while keeping held-out evaluation fixed.

## Limitations and open questions

Traffic simulation is a single-agent technical domain, so social norms, identity, and multi-agent negotiation are not tested. Autonomous task generation may also select easy or self-confirming skills without stronger curriculum audits.

## Relation to this map

SimSkill offers a reproducible memory lifecycle for future social worlds: exploration, verification, consolidation, and transfer can be evaluated separately from dialogue fluency.
