## One-line takeaway

Large preregistered replay campaigns find that a model name on a shared endpoint is not a stable measurement instrument for agent evaluation.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Shared black-box model endpoints used to score generations and gate training data.
- **Agents:** LLM observers/judges and the requests they evaluate.
- **Observations and actions:** Identical requests are replayed across windows, days, providers, and hosting modes; the judge emits rankings or labels.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Test whether repeated measurements meet preregistered reliability thresholds before a score is used as a gate.

## What the paper does

The study runs two preregistered campaigns with fixed thresholds, then probes label semantics, candidate separation, provider changes, waiting, and self-hosted kernels. It proposes a snapshot-identity ladder, design rules, and a pilot procedure for exposing an unreliable instrument early.

## Evidence and findings

Across 52,988 attempts, the authors report same-window ranking agreement of Spearman 0.400 against a 0.90 requirement and next-day byte-identical agreement of 0.78 against 0.99. They also report provider medians from 0.74 to 0.88 and little improvement from waiting; these results concern the tested shared infrastructure and are not a universal claim about all judges.

## Reusable patterns

- Freeze and identify the serving instrument, not just the model label.
- Pilot repeatability before building a benchmark gate or reward loop.
- Report noise floors and candidate gaps together, with replay artifacts.

## Limitations and open questions

The evidence targets black-box observers and a particular serving grid. The abstract does not resolve how deterministic local judges, structured checkers, or social metrics behave under the same protocol. Full preregistration and raw replay data should be checked before adopting the thresholds.

## Relation to this map

This is a critical guardrail for social-simulation claims: ratings of norm compliance, persona consistency, or collective behavior should carry instrument identity and repeatability checks alongside the agent and world configuration.
