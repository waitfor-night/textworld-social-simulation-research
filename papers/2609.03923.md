## One-line takeaway

CAPA treats meeting participation as a stateful decision about whether, when, and what to say rather than a prompt-only generation task.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Online meetings represented as a stream of observed turns and annotated idea units from the AMI corpus.
- **Agents:** A delegated LLM participant with Perceiver, Predictor, Controller, Generator, and Recalibrator modules.
- **Observations and actions:** The agent updates stance, coverage, and floor state; it predicts the next turn and either speaks or remains silent.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Recover a principal’s ideas at appropriate moments while avoiding hallucinated contributions.

## What the paper does

CAPA updates a structured meeting state after every turn, forecasts what will happen next, selects a proposition and speaking decision, renders it in the participant’s style, and feeds judge verdicts back into the state. The evaluation scores episode-level timing and content around actual idea units.

## Evidence and findings

On 137 AMI meetings, the authors report silence falling from 51.4% to 2.5%, credited recovery rising from 26.1 to 52.2, and hallucination at 0.6%; judge agreement with human annotations is reported as Cohen’s kappa 0.71. The paper is accepted at EMNLP 2026 Main, but the results remain corpus- and protocol-specific.

## Reusable patterns

- Keep a compact, explicit interaction state separate from raw context.
- Decompose participation into recognition, timing, selection, and realization.
- Attribute residual failures to modules and evaluate at episode level.

## Limitations and open questions

Meetings are cooperative and mostly two-sided; norms, strategic silence, and private beliefs in larger populations may require additional state. The judge-based parts also warrant the repeatability checks raised by current evaluation papers.

## Relation to this map

CAPA is a concrete scheduling template for social text worlds: an agent’s role and memory should feed a controller that decides when an action belongs in the shared episode.
