## One-line takeaway

Serving adapters can silently erase valid tool calls, making an agent look incapable and even training a reinforcement-learning loop on a trajectory that never executed.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** BFCL and tau-bench interfaces, plus a tool-using training loop and vLLM-style serving stacks.
- **Agents:** Several code-agent model families and their tool-call adapters.
- **Observations and actions:** The model emits structured calls; templates, parsers, schemas, and executors either preserve or censor them before environment feedback.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Compare identical weights, cases, decoding, and seeds while changing only the interface contract.

## What the paper does

The study uses controlled adapter swaps and a scale probe to separate emitted calls from server-parsed and executed calls. It also checks a training loop and releases a short preflight that asserts a non-empty, correctly named, parseable tool call under a positive-control setting.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report BFCL v4 scores of 0.00 versus 0.96/0.19 under adapter changes, tau-bench parsed calls moving from 0 to 636 and tasks reaching execution from 0 to 103, and 45/115 complete calls in one training setup with none accepted. Repairing parsing restored calls but did not yield a statistically significant pass-rate gain in the reported evaluation.

## Reusable patterns

- Log emitted, parsed, accepted, executed, and observed stages separately.
- Run a minimal interface preflight before every benchmark or RL run.
- Preserve raw requests and adapter versions so trajectories can be re-parsed.

## Limitations and open questions

The experiments focus on tool-call interfaces and selected benchmarks; social-world action schemas may fail in different ways. A passing preflight does not prove that later observations or rewards are correct. The repository’s errata should be read before reusing individual numbers.

## Relation to this map

Text-world simulations need the same funnel accounting: a proposed social action must be distinguished from an accepted event and an observed consequence, or apparent behavioral differences may be interface artifacts.
