## One-line takeaway

The paper frames a text world model as a transition model that predicts the next textual state after an agent acts in a web, terminal, API, or dialogue environment.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Textual web, terminal, API, and user-reply environments.
- **Agents:** LLM-based agents that observe text and propose actions.
- **Observations and actions:** A textual state plus a candidate action is mapped to a predicted next state or response.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Support planning, interaction, and evaluation under sequential dynamics.

## What the paper does

This work is a systematic review of text world models. It organizes their foundations, construction methods, applications, and evaluation questions, and uses the agent–world interface to distinguish an agent policy from the model of the environment’s transitions.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract identifies the central problem as an agent–world gap: an agent can be capable while lacking a reliable model of how its actions change the world. The paper is useful as a taxonomy and evaluation checklist; the details of individual systems should be verified from the full text and linked implementations.

## Reusable patterns

- Store textual states and actions as first-class trajectory objects.
- Evaluate transition fidelity separately from downstream agent success.
- Test partial observability, compositional generalization, counterfactual actions, and uncertainty calibration.

## Limitations and open questions

The review is broader than social simulation and does not by itself specify a social mechanism, authority model, or memory architecture. A social text world model still needs explicit rules for visibility, belief, event causality, and multi-agent scheduling.

## Relation to this map

This is a core conceptual anchor for separating `text environment` from `text world model`: the former exposes observations/actions and feedback, while the latter predicts or represents the transition dynamics behind that interface.
