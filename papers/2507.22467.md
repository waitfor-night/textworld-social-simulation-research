## One-line takeaway

The paper uses structured multi-agent scenarios to study how LLM agents exhibit conformity, polarization, and fragmentation under social influence.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Structured social-influence simulation scenarios.
- **Agents:** LLM-based multi-agents with differing model families or capabilities.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents observe other agents’ positions and produce socially influenced responses or updates.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Measure emergent influence dynamics across conformity, polarization, and fragmentation settings.

## What the paper does

It constructs controlled simulations for several classic social-influence outcomes and compares the behavior of language models, including smaller and reasoning-optimized variants.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports that smaller models display higher conformity, while reasoning-optimized models are more resistant to social influence. These are model-behavior findings under the paper’s protocol, not general claims about human psychology.

## Reusable patterns

- Turn named social phenomena into explicit scenario-level metrics.
- Compare model scale and reasoning configuration as experimental factors.
- Record initial positions, exposures, updates, and final group structure.

## Limitations and open questions

Controlled scenarios may overfit to the prompting protocol. The work does not by itself solve calibration to human longitudinal data, hidden beliefs, or external-world events.

## Relation to this map

Useful baseline for a social-diffusion sandbox, especially for testing whether an implementation produces conformity or polarization for the intended reasons rather than as an artifact of the prompt.
