## One-line takeaway

CAMO turns a macro-level emergence question into computable factors, learns a compact upstream causal structure, and probes it with simulator-internal counterfactuals.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** LLM-agent simulations with recorded micro behaviors and macro outcomes.
- **Agents:** LLM-based social agents.
- **Observations and actions:** Simulation records are converted into candidate factors; interventions or counterfactual probes test causal influence.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Explain a selected macro target through a minimal upstream subgraph or Markov boundary.

## What the paper does

The framework uses agents to translate a mechanistic hypothesis into measurable variables, discover a compact causal representation around a macro outcome, and conduct counterfactual probing inside the simulator. The abstract reports four emergent settings.

## Evidence and findings

The main contribution is methodological: it makes the micro-to-macro bridge an explicit analysis object instead of relying on post-hoc narrative explanations. Exact datasets, baselines, and effect-size claims should be read from the full paper.

## Reusable patterns

- Define macro targets before collecting explanations.
- Log measurable micro events instead of only final prose.
- Use interventions and counterfactual replays to distinguish correlation from a simulator mechanism.
- Prefer a compact causal graph that can be inspected and reproduced.

## Limitations and open questions

Simulator-internal counterfactuals can reveal the model’s mechanism without validating that the mechanism exists in human society. Factor extraction may also inherit LLM interpretation bias, and causal claims depend on intervention semantics and random seeds.

## Relation to this map

This is a strong candidate for the evaluation layer: `event log → factor extraction → causal graph → intervention/replay`. It complements outcome-level calibration with a test of why a social pattern appeared.
