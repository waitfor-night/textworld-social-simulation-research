## One-line takeaway

The Civilization Framework makes a persistent ledger and human “sovereign” the addressable party, then tests how message order and verification affect delegated authority.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Asynchronous communication between civilizations, each composed of a human principal, a persistent ledger, and interchangeable agents.
- **Agents:** Online agents that read and write their civilization’s ledger and remote agents acting for another ledger.
- **Observations and actions:** Embassy Protocol messages arrive at a resident endpoint; signed credentials and commitment records constrain actions.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Preserve context across agent handoffs and prevent first-arriving claims from receiving unearned authority.

## What the paper does

The paper defines ledger commitments as ground truth, separates agent authority from civilization reputation, and studies a temporal-weight effect in a preregistered communication experiment. It also describes an implementation of the intra-civilization layer.

## Evidence and findings

Across 1,908 trials with one frontier model, the abstract reports an incorrect upstream claim capturing 54.2% of answers without verification versus 4.2% with full verification; arrival after a sealed answer yields 31.6%. The registration’s tool-call budget condition failed, so the authors classify all results as exploratory.

## Reusable patterns

- Give commitments a durable, auditable home independent of whichever agent is online.
- Separate identity, authority, reputation, and accessible memory.
- Treat message ordering and verification as experimental factors.

## Limitations and open questions

The experiment uses one model, prompt shells are not length-matched, and the failed budget check limits inference. The framework’s “sovereign” abstraction also needs testing in populations with competing institutions and partial information.

## Relation to this map

This offers a concrete identity-and-ledger design for social simulations where agents rotate but obligations persist. It complements authoritative world-state and memory-provenance patterns already in the map.
