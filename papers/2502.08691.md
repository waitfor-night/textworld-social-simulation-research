## One-line takeaway

AgentSociety presents a large-scale simulator with more than 10,000 agents and 5 million interactions for studying behaviors and interventions across several social issues.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Social, urban, and external-shock scenarios.
- **Agents:** More than 10,000 LLM-driven generative agents.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents interact with one another and with environmental conditions over simulated time.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Study polarization, inflammatory messages, universal basic income, hurricanes, and urban sustainability through surveys, interventions, and mechanism analyses.

## What the paper does

The simulator is designed to scale multi-agent interaction and expose a common platform for social experiments. The reported application set spans opinion phenomena, policy interventions, natural hazards, and urban behavior.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports over 10,000 agents and 5 million interactions. It presents the system as a tool for advancing understanding, but the abstract alone does not establish external predictive accuracy across the listed domains.

## Reusable patterns

- Separate scenario configuration from the general simulation engine.
- Support interventions and exogenous shocks as reproducible inputs.
- Collect both aggregate outcomes and individual trajectories.
- Treat scaling as an engineering variable that can change emergent behavior.

## Limitations and open questions

Large scale does not resolve agent validity, memory drift, prompt sensitivity, or calibration. It is important to test whether aggregate results survive model, seed, network, and scheduling changes.
