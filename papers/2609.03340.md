## One-line takeaway

PlanFence validates the records that can affect a pending action, catching stale plans even when an agent has read the latest shared facts.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Distributed live workflows with shared records and post-plan revisions.
- **Agents:** A planner, other agents that commit updates, and an executor.
- **Observations and actions:** Plans cite source records; before an external action, the executor checks only relevant dependencies and either replans or blocks.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Prevent an action derived from an obsolete requirement while limiting synchronization overhead.

## What the paper does

The proposed protocol treats plan validity as distinct from state freshness. It attaches exact record references to a plan and scopes validation to the action’s dependency set. Controlled replay compares this policy with freshness-only execution and proactive synchronization.

## Evidence and findings

In 30 live workflows with a post-plan revision, the authors report that freshness-only execution acted on an obsolete plan in every task, while PlanFence completed all tasks without an invalid action. Replay indicates proactive synchronization is cheaper at low churn, whereas dependency validation scales better with churn and keyspace size.

## Reusable patterns

- Bind every action to the evidence and requirements that authorized it.
- Validate at the commit boundary, not only when reading shared state.
- Report safety outcomes separately from coordination cost and task completion.

## Limitations and open questions

The workflows are controlled and the abstract explicitly does not claim general accuracy gains. Social worlds add hidden beliefs, informal commitments, and ambiguous dependencies that may require richer scopes than record IDs.

## Relation to this map

PlanFence is a useful event-sourcing primitive: a social action should carry the belief and world-state versions it relied on, enabling safe replay and principled invalidation.
