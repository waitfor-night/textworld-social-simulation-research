## One-line takeaway

Full-duplex agents often handle explicit turn rules better than rules implied by a persona, revealing a gap between role understanding and situated action timing.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Real-time spoken interactions with overlaps, backchannels, interruptions, and floor transfer.
- **Agents:** Six speech systems conditioned by eight assistant roles.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents listen, take or yield the floor, backchannel, interrupt, and produce persona-consistent content.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Follow explicit, persona-implied, combined, or conflicting instructions at the correct conversational moment.

## What the paper does

DSB-IFEval contains 1,038 test cases and five conditioning protocols. It scores floor management with a deterministic Instruction Adherence Score and persona content with a judged Persona Adherence Score.

## Evidence and findings

Across six systems, the authors report persona-only adherence drops of 9.7% for F-Actor and 4.5% for PersonaPlex, while other systems preserve content but fail to adapt floor behavior. Systems also struggle to override a persona under a safety conflict. The work is listed as under submission.

## Reusable patterns

- Score role-consistent content separately from timing and interaction control.
- Include implicit-role and conflict conditions, not only explicit instructions.
- Use deterministic event timing for turn-taking metrics and human checks for content.

## Limitations and open questions

This is a voice-domain benchmark; text-world agents may express timing through action scheduling rather than audio overlap. The abstract does not establish transfer to multi-party social populations or persistent identity.

## Relation to this map

The benchmark supports a two-layer role model: persona constraints shape what an agent wants to say, while the environment decides when a contribution is admissible and socially situated.
