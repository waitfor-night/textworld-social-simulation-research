## One-line takeaway

DRACO converts a trajectory-level rubric into differentiated per-step learning signals for outcome-blind long-horizon agent training.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** AppWorld and out-of-domain Tau-Bench tool-use tasks without a complete programmatic success signal.
- **Agents:** GRPO-trained language agents and a rubric-generating judge.
- **Observations and actions:** The agent produces multi-step trajectories; rubric citations identify which steps support each judged criterion.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Train from completed trajectories, dynamically generate criteria, and redistribute the scalar judgment over responsible steps.

## What the paper does

DRACO generates prompt- and trajectory-specific rubrics, scores them after a rollout, and uses the judge’s cited evidence to reallocate the resulting advantage without adding a learned attribution module. Ablations vary static versus dynamic rubrics, credit redistribution, and judge choice.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports gains of 15.9 points over the base model and 5.3 over sparse-ground-truth GRPO on AppWorld, plus 5.3 points over the base model on out-of-domain Tau-Bench without a frontier judge. These are benchmark-specific author claims; the method still depends on rubric quality and citation coverage.

## Reusable patterns

- Keep trajectory-level evaluation while preserving step-level credit links.
- Make reward attribution inspectable rather than treating a long rollout as one indivisible score.
- Test transfer to a different environment and judge configuration.

## Limitations and open questions

Rubric judges can introduce bias or miss social side effects, and cited steps may be correlated with fluent explanations rather than causal progress. It is unknown whether redistribution helps norm learning, relationship maintenance, or other non-verifiable social objectives.

## Relation to this map

The credit graph is a useful training/evaluation primitive for long social episodes: attach outcomes to the actions and observations that produced them, while retaining uncertainty about causal responsibility.
