## One-line takeaway

SILICA tests agent societies for human-distribution agreement, invariance to rule-preserving presentation changes, and evidence that observed dynamics come from interaction rather than memorized priors.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Five social or economic environments paired with published human reference distributions, re-rendered rules, and payoff variants.
- **Agents:** Twelve open-weight language models instantiated as interacting society members.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents choose environment actions and communicate under each environment's information and payoff rules; the benchmark also perturbs action labels and schedules offers.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Compare starting and end-state behavior with human anchors, test order and apparatus sensitivity, and probe whether conventions arise through negotiation.

## What the paper does

SILICA defines a certification ladder and runs the same societies under rule-preserving re-renderings and incentives that point away from remembered outcomes. It reports separate checks for individual choices, population trajectories, and convention formation.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report 9,115 model runs and 150 classical baselines: eight of eleven evaluated models fall within the first-round public-goods equivalence margin, while none matches end-state contributions or the human cooperation corridor. Swapping two action labels lowers one model's cooperation by 58 points; fixed offer schedules reveal inconsistent incentive thresholds, and conventions often follow shared naming priors until those priors are disrupted. The linked GitHub, Zenodo, and OSF artifacts improve replayability, but these results remain exploratory author claims pending independent reruns.

## Reusable patterns

- Anchor social claims to distributions and trajectories, not a single plausible episode.
- Apply presentation-preserving perturbations and payoff reversals before interpreting emergence.
- Separate first-round fit, end-state fit, interaction dependence, and order sensitivity.

## Limitations and open questions

Although the paper links code, transcripts, a reproducible release, and an OSF plan/anchor deposit, independent replication is not reported and the human-anchor sources remain partly external and non-frozen. Results may depend on model sampling, prompts, action labels, and the chosen equivalence margins; transfer to richer text worlds remains open.
