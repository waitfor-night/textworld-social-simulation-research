## One-line takeaway

trajectory-judge uses deterministic single-step fault injection to show that outcome-only evaluators miss silent process failures and unsupported final claims.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** A deterministic tool-using support-desk environment with scripted ground-truth behavior.
- **Agents:** A scripted oracle policy, tool-using agent trajectories, and five evaluators: programmatic rules, outcome-only, two step-rubric judges, and a self-consistency ensemble.
- **Observations and actions:** Judges can inspect requests, tool steps, and/or final replies; a fault injector changes exactly one known step and labels whether the customer-visible outcome survives.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Detect, localize, type, and calibrate faults across clean, silent-fault, and loud-fault trajectories while reporting evaluation cost.

## What the paper does

The benchmark compares outcome-only and process-aware judging over 400 trajectories. It explicitly separates faults whose visible outcome survives from those that break the final result, then adds a final-message stress test with an invented promise.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report 84% loud-fault recall and 45% silent-fault recall for the outcome-only judge, with 33% false alarms on correct trajectories. A step-rubric judge reaches 77% silent-fault recall with zero false alarms at roughly three times the cost. No tested judge reliably reads the final reply, allowing an invented promise to evade the process checks. The arXiv record links raw verdicts and an offline rebuild pipeline; results remain preprint claims.

## Reusable patterns

- Inject known single-step failures and stratify them by outcome survival.
- Report detection, localization, fault type, calibration, and cost separately.
- Audit the final message as a distinct artifact rather than assuming trajectory correctness implies safe output.

## Limitations and open questions

The support-desk rules and fault taxonomy are narrow, and scripted ground truth may make detection easier than in open social worlds. Transfer to hidden beliefs, delayed norm violations, multi-agent collusion, and adversarially polished final messages needs testing.
