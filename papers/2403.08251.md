## One-line takeaway

CRSEC decomposes social-norm emergence into creation/representation, spreading, evaluation, and compliance modules in a Smallville-style agent society.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** A Smallville-like sandbox with generative agents.
- **Agents:** Generative social agents that communicate and respond to norms.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents create, communicate, evaluate, and comply with candidate norms.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Study how norms become established and whether they reduce conflicts.

## What the paper does

The CRSEC architecture names four modules: Creation and Representation, Spreading, Evaluation, and Compliance. The design makes a candidate norm an explicit object that can be communicated, assessed, and followed instead of leaving social regularities implicit in prose.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports experiments in a Smallville environment and a human evaluation with 30 evaluators. It claims that the architecture helps establish social norms and reduce conflicts; this entry records the claim without treating it as evidence of human social validity.

## Reusable patterns

- Represent norms separately from individual memories and messages.
- Track norm proposals, exposure, acceptance, violations, and sanctions.
- Evaluate both norm prevalence and conflict outcomes.
- Preserve the distinction between descriptive regularity and prescriptive rule.

## Limitations and open questions

The small sandbox and limited human evaluation leave open questions about scale, cultural variation, incentive compatibility, and whether agents comply for the intended reasons.

## Relation to this map

Directly useful for the social-rules layer. It suggests adding explicit norm objects and violation events to the world state, rather than asking a narrator to infer norms after the fact.
