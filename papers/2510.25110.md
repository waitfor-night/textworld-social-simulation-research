## One-line takeaway

DEBATE evaluates whether role-playing LLM agents reproduce both the messages and the evolving private beliefs of human discussion groups.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Multi-round group discussions with public messages and private Likert-scale beliefs.
- **Agents:** 2,788 cleaned participants organized into 697 groups across 107 topics; LLM agents simulate participants.
- **Observations and actions:** Public message history plus private stance/belief state; agents produce the next message and update their stance.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Evaluate next-message prediction and full opinion dynamics, including convergence and stance alignment.

## What the paper does

The benchmark compares role-playing agents against human discussion trajectories. It evaluates seven LLMs and includes supervised fine-tuning experiments intended to improve stance alignment and reduce convergence error.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports that zero-shot role-playing-agent groups converge more strongly than human groups. Fine-tuning improves stance alignment and reduces convergence error, but important discrepancies remain.

## Reusable patterns

- Evaluate public language and private belief trajectories separately.
- Use group-level dynamics metrics, not just turn-level text similarity.
- Compare convergence, polarization, and disagreement retention against human trajectories.
- Preserve participant identity and topic context across all rounds.

## Limitations and open questions

Human discussion data can contain selection and measurement bias, while Likert beliefs are only a coarse state representation. Benchmark performance may not transfer to open-ended societies, non-discussion actions, or long-horizon life simulation.

## Relation to this map

This should be a primary evaluation reference for any social text environment that claims to model influence, disagreement, or polarization. It maps naturally onto separate `public message`, `private belief`, and `group aggregate` state channels.
