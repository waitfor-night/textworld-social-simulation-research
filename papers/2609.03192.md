## One-line takeaway

Interventions in a persistent simulated settlement separate properties enforced by the institution and world from behaviors that depend on agent cognition.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** A persistent settlement with an append-only authoritative ledger that adjudicates attempted acts against world state.
- **Agents:** Native simulated minds, reset or ablated minds, and a substituted frozen frontier-model panel.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents receive evidence and beliefs, propose acts, and receive typed acceptance or refusal from the institutional ledger.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Localize reliability claims by holding either cognition or enforcement fixed while changing the other.

## What the paper does

The authors predeclare interventions on evidence provenance, belief availability, physical-evidence legibility, and cognition. They then track witness effects, false testimony, duplicate work, duties, accepted reality, and completion claims over replayable trajectories.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports that one falsehood costs trusting runs about 900 futile actions, while five declared properties remain invariant: singular accepted reality, typed refusal, durable duties, no duplicate acceptance, and no false completion (2,581 substituted-panel claims, none false). The authors limit the claim to one designed world with no adversarial optimizer.

## Reusable patterns

- Separate mind, institution, and world as independently intervenable layers.
- Make accepted history append-only and replayable.
- Report both failed predictions and invariant safety properties.

## Limitations and open questions

One world and one institutional design cannot establish general reliability. Population-level effects, strategic gaming of the ledger, and social norms outside formal duties remain untested.
