## One-line takeaway

DNative-Twin stores typed decision trajectories in a graph-native twin so an agent action can be replayed, inspected, and tested under controlled changes.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Enterprise decision processes represented by observed state, paths, authority, and replay contracts.
- **Agents:** Agentic decision systems and an analysis/replay service.
- **Observations and actions:** Logs are converted to typed graph nodes and edges; replay re-executes decisions under injected changes or verification states.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Reconstruct why a decision occurred and expose unresolved divergence rather than only recording its outcome.

## What the paper does

The framework combines a graph-native trajectory store with digital-twin replay and explicit verification state. The reported evaluation uses three public process logs and 300 injected instances, comparing plain logs, replay contracts, and verification.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report unresolved-divergence recall rising from 0 to 0.667 with replay-contract state and to 1.0 with verification. Median latency rises from 0.794 to 8.889 seconds at 500–5,000 cases; the held-out set contains no critical-class instance, limiting the strongest safety conclusion.

## Reusable patterns

- Represent authority, evidence, and action dependencies as typed graph data.
- Make replay and verification state explicit rather than inferred from prose.
- Report detection gains together with latency and held-out class coverage.

## Limitations and open questions

The evaluation is enterprise-process specific and does not test open-ended social behavior or hidden beliefs. Graph schemas for informal norms and conversational commitments remain to be designed.

## Relation to this map

DNative-Twin is a useful implementation reference for event-sourced text worlds: every social action should be reconstructable from its observed state, authority, and evidence path.
