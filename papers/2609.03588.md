## One-line takeaway

KC-Bench tests whether an agent can reconcile parametric knowledge, user input, and changing world observations before acting.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Stateful simulated tools with multi-turn user interactions and deterministic assertions.
- **Agents:** LLM agents evaluated with a user simulator and a natural-language evaluator.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents receive conflicting facts, identity cues, or temporal sources, then decide whether and how to call tools.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Detect and resolve world-knowledge, input, and multi-source temporal conflicts without propagating them into execution.

## What the paper does

The benchmark screens 238 tasks from more than 1,000 generated candidates, combines automated assertions with human trajectory verification, and evaluates nine models across the three conflict families.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report substantial cross-domain variation: no tested model handled factual correction, identity consistency, and temporal conflict reliably across all settings. Missed conflicts can flow into tool calls and synthetic protected-data paths. The benchmark isolates model behavior rather than ranking complete agent frameworks.

## Reusable patterns

- Make conflict types explicit and replayable in a stateful environment.
- Assert both the decision and the downstream tool/state consequences.
- Pair deterministic checks with human inspection of trajectories.

## Limitations and open questions

The task suite is synthetic and the abstract gives limited detail on population or relationship dynamics. Social worlds need conflicts involving private beliefs, norms, and authority, not only factual consistency.

## Relation to this map

KC-Bench is a ready evaluation pattern for text worlds: inject contradictory observations and verify whether agents preserve identity, provenance, and world-state boundaries before committing events.
