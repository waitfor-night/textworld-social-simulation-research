## One-line takeaway

This position paper argues that LLMs expand the expressive range of agent-based social simulation while making validation, grounding, bias control, and reproducibility more difficult.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Agent-based models of social systems, including hybrid classical/LLM simulators.
- **Agents:** Software agents driven by SLMs or LLMs, possibly combined with conventional ABM rules.
- **Observations and actions:** Natural-language perception and action embedded in a structured simulation loop.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Explore architectures, scalability, validation, and the epistemic status of simulation results.

## What the paper does

The authors review opportunities and limitations of integrating LLMs into ABM and discuss hybrid approaches, including use with platforms such as GAMA or NetLogo. A recurring distinction is between operational usefulness and explanatory or predictive validity.

## Evidence and findings

The paper is a position/survey contribution rather than a single benchmark. Its main value for this map is the explicit warning that realistic language and plausible behavior do not automatically establish valid social mechanisms or real-world prediction.

## Reusable patterns

- Keep classical rules where they provide interpretable constraints.
- Define calibration targets before claiming realism.
- Report seeds, prompts, model versions, retrieval context, and simulator versions.
- Separate exploratory world-building from explanatory or predictive use.

## Limitations and open questions

As a broad position paper, it does not settle which hybrid architecture works best for a given phenomenon. It also leaves substantial room for concrete, reproducible benchmark protocols.
