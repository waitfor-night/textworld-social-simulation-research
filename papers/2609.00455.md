## One-line takeaway

Belief-Based World Models expose what is known and uncertain alongside simulated action consequences, giving an LLM policy an uncertainty-aware interface under partial observability.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Partially observable interactive tasks evaluated with ALFWorld and ScienceWorld implementations linked by the authors.
- **Agents:** LLM policies paired with a belief-maintaining world-model interface, optionally alongside a simulation-based world model.
- **Observations and actions:** The policy receives observations, queries a belief over current state, simulates candidate actions, and then commits an action to the environment.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Improve long-horizon decisions by distinguishing known, unknown, and uncertain state before selecting actions.

## What the paper does

The paper argues that a simulator-only interface conflates an action's predicted consequences with uncertainty about the present state. It introduces a queryable belief representation and compares belief access, simulation access, and their combination in controlled agent conditions.

## Evidence and findings

The authors report that exposing beliefs improves task performance under partial observability and remains complementary to simulation. The arXiv record links public reproduction code and names ALFWorld and ScienceWorld; the numerical results and ablations remain author-reported until independently rerun.

## Reusable patterns

- Represent epistemic state as a first-class projection rather than burying uncertainty in narrative text.
- Keep current-state belief queries separate from counterfactual action simulation.
- Evaluate information access and action quality as distinct intervention conditions.

## Limitations and open questions

The record focuses on task-solving environments rather than social worlds, so beliefs about other agents, private information, and norms are not yet tested. The quality and calibration of the belief model itself, leakage between hidden state and queries, and robustness across model backbones need controlled audit.

## Relation to this map

BB-WM supplies a concrete uncertainty layer for text-world and social-simulation designs. It complements executable state and replay records by specifying what each agent is allowed to know before acting.
