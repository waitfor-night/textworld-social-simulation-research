## One-line takeaway

The paper proposes a prospective audit for deciding whether a social mechanism observed in a small agent group will survive population scaling.

## Research object

- **World or environment:** Agent societies with mechanisms such as reciprocity, consensus, punishment, and gossip.
- **Agents:** Interacting language-model agents whose messages and social information can be scaled in reach and lifetime.
- **Observations and actions:** Agents exchange mechanism-relevant signals and act; the audit tracks activation frequency, information use, and measurement artifacts.
- **Goals and interaction protocol:** Predict mechanism failure boundaries before running costly large populations, then test the prediction on controlled executions.

## What the paper does

The authors define an audit that asks whether a mechanism can activate often enough, whether agents use the information it supplies, and whether the measurement itself creates an apparent scale effect. Experiments vary structural terms and message expression across population sizes.

## Evidence and findings

The abstract reports that one structural term can decide whether reciprocity, consensus, or punishment survives scaling, while gossip failure depends on message reach and lifetime. Predictions made before execution held on third-party code and a second model family, with one failed prediction exposing a boundary.

## Reusable patterns

- Pre-register mechanism activation and information-use checks before scaling.
- Treat message lifetime, reach, and wording as causal variables.
- Use independent implementations and record failed predictions, not only successes.

## Limitations and open questions

The mechanisms and agent societies are controlled abstractions; transfer to open-ended text worlds, heterogeneous identities, and institutional feedback is unknown. The audit also needs cost-aware guidance for very large populations.
